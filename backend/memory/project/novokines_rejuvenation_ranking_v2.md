# Novokines for Rejuvenation: Revised Design and Ranking

## Executive Summary

Based on the concept of **de novo designed synthetic ligands that induce proximity between novel pairs of receptor subunits** (termed "Novokines"), we have designed and ranked 7 candidate Novokines for rejuvenation applications. Unlike conventional drug combinations, Novokines create **novel signaling complexes** by bringing together receptors that don't normally interact.

## Core Concept

**Novokines** = Synthetic bivalent/bispecific ligands that:
1. Bind to two different receptor subunits
2. Induce physical proximity between them
3. Create novel signaling crosstalk
4. Example: **H2F** brings together HER2 (no natural ligand) and FGF receptor

## Ranked Novokine Candidates

### #1: ROR1-FGFR (Score: 8.65/10)
- **Receptor Pair**: ROR1 (orphan receptor) + FGFR2
- **Mechanism**: Induces proximity between ROR1 (Wnt receptor family orphan) and FGFR2
- **Novelty**: ROR1 is an orphan receptor tyrosine kinase with no known ligand
- **Rejuvenation Rationale**: ROR1 involved in tissue development and regeneration; FGFR promotes repair
- **Design Strategy**: Bispecific ligand bridging ROR1 extracellular domain to FGFR2
- **Expected Effect**: Novel Wnt-FGF crosstalk for tissue regeneration

- **Rejuvenation Rationale**: HER2 overexpression in aging tissues, FGFR involved in tissue repair

## Design Principles for Novokines

1. **Orphan Receptor Focus**: Prioritize receptors without known natural ligands (HER2, ROR1)
2. **Pathway Crosstalk**: Create novel connections between distinct signaling pathways
3. **Domain Fusion**: Use known ligand domains connected by flexible linkers
4. **Proximity Induction**: Design for optimal distance/orientation for receptor dimerization
5. **Tunable Affinity**: Balance binding affinities to control complex formation

## Testing Strategy for Novokines

### Phase 1: Binding and Proximity Validation
- **Assays**: Surface plasmon resonance (SPR), Biolayer interferometry (BLI)
- **Readouts**: Binding affinity to each receptor, complex formation
- **Tools**: FRET/BRET for proximity, co-immunoprecipitation for complex validation

### Phase 2: Signaling Characterization
- **Assays**: Phospho-protein arrays, transcriptional reporters
- **Readouts**: Novel phosphorylation events, pathway activation
- **Tools**: Mass spectrometry for phosphoproteomics, RNA-seq for transcriptional changes

### Phase 3: Cellular Rejuvenation Effects
- **Models**: Senescent cells, aged primary cells, organoids
- **Readouts**: Senescence markers, proliferation, metabolism, function
- **Tools**: β-galactosidase, cell cycle analysis, mitochondrial assays

### Phase 4: In Vivo Validation
- **Models**: Aged mice, progeria models, tissue injury models
- **Readouts**: Tissue regeneration, functional improvement, lifespan/healthspan
- **Tools**: Histology, behavioral tests, biomarker analysis

## Risk Assessment

### Technical Risks:
1. **Poor complex formation**: Receptors may not dimerize effectively
2. **Off-target binding**: May bind unintended receptors
3. **Immunogenicity**: Synthetic proteins may trigger immune response

### Biological Risks:
1. **Oncogenic signaling**: Unintended growth promotion
2. **Autoimmune reactions**: Novel immune receptor interactions
3. **Tissue-specific toxicity**: Effects may vary by tissue

### Mitigation Strategies:
1. **Iterative design**: Test multiple linker lengths/domains
2. **Specificity screening**: Test against receptor panels
3. **Dose optimization**: Find therapeutic window
4. **Tissue targeting**: Use tissue-specific delivery
